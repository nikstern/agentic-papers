# Summary
Open Problems in Cooperative AI frames cooperation as a distinct AI research agenda spanning machine-machine and human-machine interaction, with attention to both individual capabilities and population-level institutions.

# Why It Matters
It gives the vault a broad foundation for asking what multi-agent systems should optimize beyond task completion and connects modern agent design to game theory, social choice, human interaction, and institutional design.

# Method / Setup
The position paper synthesizes work across multi-agent systems, reinforcement learning, game theory, social choice, human-machine interaction, and the behavioral sciences. It organizes cooperative capabilities around understanding, communication, commitment, and institutions, and distinguishes an individual-agent perspective from a social-planner perspective.

# Key Claims
- Cooperation should be treated as an explicit AI research problem rather than a side effect of general capability.
- Useful cooperative agents need capabilities for understanding other actors, establishing common ground, making credible commitments, and operating within or improving institutions.
- Evaluation should cover both machine-machine and human-machine cooperation under common and conflicting interests.
- Cooperative capabilities can also enable exclusion, collusion, deception, or coercion, so welfare effects and governance must be studied directly.

# Limitations
- The paper is a research agenda rather than an implemented system or benchmark.
- Its taxonomy is intentionally broad and does not specify a single operational evaluation protocol.
- Many proposed capabilities and institutional interventions require empirical validation in realistic mixed human-machine populations.
