# Multi-Agent Foundations

## Definition

Foundational work on cooperation, multi-agent learning, partial observability,
partner modeling, and decentralized execution that informs the design and
evaluation of modern LLM-based multi-agent systems.

## Core Questions

- How do multiple adaptive agents change the learning and control problem?
- What information may be centralized during training or evaluation but
  unavailable during decentralized execution?
- How do agents coordinate when observations, beliefs, and conventions differ?
- Why can a policy that coordinates well in self-play fail with a new partner?
- Which classical multi-agent concepts transfer to LLM-agent orchestration,
  and which require different evidence?

## Scope

This is an LLM-centered bridge, not a comprehensive classical multi-agent
systems or multi-agent reinforcement learning curriculum. Contract-net
allocation, formal consensus, mechanism design, and epistemic logic remain
useful follow-on gaps.

## Suggested Reading Path

1. Cooperation as the research problem.
2. Non-stationarity and centralized training with decentralized execution.
3. Partial observability, beliefs, conventions, and partner modeling.
